## 1. Press release

### Zoldify launches a second-hand marketplace where the buyer's money is never sent to a stranger

**Payment is held by the platform until the buyer confirms the item arrived —
closing the gap that makes deposit fraud possible on classified-ads sites.**

Ho Chi Minh City — Zoldify today opened a marketplace for buying and selling
second-hand and surplus goods between individuals. Unlike classified-ads
platforms, where the buyer transfers money directly to a stranger's bank account,
Zoldify holds every payment in escrow and releases it to the seller only after the
buyer confirms the item was received.

**The problem.** Buying used online in Vietnam today means paying a stranger and
hoping. Classified-ads platforms introduce a buyer to a seller, host the
conversation, and then step out of the way at exactly the moment money changes
hands. A seller can ask for a deposit to hold an item, take it, delete the listing
and disappear — and because the transfer went bank-to-bank, the platform has no
record of it and nothing to refund. Buyers respond rationally: they refuse to pay
first, sellers refuse to ship first, and the trade never happens.

**What Zoldify does.** The buyer pays through the platform. The money goes into an
escrow account held by Zoldify, not to the seller. The seller sees that payment is
secured and ships. When the buyer confirms the item arrived, Zoldify releases the
money to the seller's balance, minus a commission on the sale. If the item never
arrives or is not what was described, the money is still in escrow and can be
refunded — because it never left.

Every movement of money is recorded as a double-entry accounting transaction. For
any amount in the system, there is a complete record of where it came from and
which account it belongs to at that moment. Nothing is a "balance" that can drift.

> "Every second-hand marketplace tells sellers to be careful and buyers to be
> careful. That advice does nothing, because the risk is not carelessness — it is
> that the platform never touches the money. We built the money layer first and
> the catalogue second."
> — Project lead, Zoldify

**How it works.**

1. The seller lists an item and declares its condition — *new, like new, good,*
   or *fair.*
2. The buyer pays through the platform. Funds settle into Zoldify's escrow, not
   the seller's account.
3. The seller ships, knowing the payment is already secured.
4. The buyer confirms the item arrived.
5. Zoldify releases the money to the seller and takes its commission.

**Availability.** Zoldify is available on the web and as an app for Android and
iOS. Listing is free; Zoldify charges a commission only on completed sales.

## 2. External FAQ — questions users will ask

**Q1. Where is my money between paying and receiving the item?**
In an escrow account held by the platform. It is not in the seller's account and
the seller cannot withdraw it. Your order page shows the amount and its state at
all times.

**Q2. What if the item never arrives?**
The money is still in escrow, so it can be returned to you. Nothing has to be
clawed back from the seller, because the seller was never paid.

**Q3. What if the item arrives but is not what was described?**
Do not confirm receipt. Confirming receipt is the action that releases the money,
and it is deliberately the buyer's action alone — the seller cannot mark an item
as delivered on your behalf.

**Q4. How long does the seller wait to be paid?**
Until you confirm receipt. For a typical delivery that is a few days.

**Q5. What does it cost?**
Listing is free. Zoldify takes a commission on the sale price, deducted when the
escrow is released. The buyer pays the listed price plus shipping — the commission
comes out of the seller's side.

**Q6. Can I sell something brand new and unopened?**
Yes. Condition is a declared field with four levels, and *new* is one of them.
Zoldify is for individuals selling individual items, whether the item is worn or
still sealed — not for shops selling repeatable inventory.

**Q7. How do I get my money out after selling?**
Request a withdrawal to your bank account. The amount is set aside immediately so
it cannot be spent twice, then transferred once approved.

**Q8. Is there cash on delivery?**
Yes, and it is worth understanding the trade-off: with cash on delivery no escrow
is created, so the buyer protection described above does not apply. It is the
faster option and the less protected one.

**Q9. Why should I trust Zoldify to hold the money?**
Because it is recorded rather than promised. Every movement is a double-entry
transaction; the sum of all entries is always zero and each account's balance is
derived from its entries rather than stored as an editable number. The same
payment processed twice produces one transaction, not two.

**Q10. What is Zoldify's role if there is a dispute?**
Zoldify holds the money while the dispute is open. That is the substantive
difference from a listing site: the platform can act, because it has something in
its hands.

## 3. Internal FAQ — questions the team must answer

### I1. Why not just build this on Shopee's model, or use Shopee?

Shopee already solves escrow. What it does not solve is the one-off individual
trade: its seller onboarding, stock model, and return policy assume repeatable
inventory. A person selling one used lens is not a Shopee seller in any meaningful
sense.

The honest version of the answer: **Zoldify is not a better Shopee. It applies
Shopee's payment-custody model to the segment Chợ Tốt and Facebook Marketplace
serve without any payment layer at all.** See the quadrant table in
[Problem Discovery §3](./2026-08-12-problem-discovery.md).

### I2. Holding other people's money is a regulated activity. Is this legal?

In Vietnam, providing intermediary payment services — e-wallets, payment gateways,
collection and disbursement on behalf of others — requires a licence from the State
Bank of Vietnam.

How Zoldify is arranged:

- Payments are processed by **PayOS**, a licensed payment gateway, which settles
  the funds into the project operator's bank account. Zoldify operates no e-wallet
  of its own and holds nothing outside the banking system.
- What Zoldify maintains internally is a **ledger of entitlement** — a record of
  how much of that settled balance belongs to each seller at any moment. The
  ledger decides who gets paid; it is not itself a store of money.
- Withdrawal is a payout instruction against that record, not a transfer between
  user-held wallets.

Zoldify is an academic project and does not process funds for the general public.

Answering this before being asked is much stronger than being caught by it — it
shows the team knew it was building something that sits in a regulated space.

### I3. What happens if the buyer simply never confirms receipt?

**Right now: the money stays in escrow forever.** There is no timer, no automatic
release, and no expiry anywhere in the codebase — verified 12/08/2026, no scheduled
job exists in the money or ordering modules.

This is a genuine hole, not a hypothetical one. A buyer who receives an item and
then forgets to press a button leaves the seller unpaid indefinitely, and there is
currently no path out other than an admin acting manually.

The standard fix is an automatic release after a fixed window from delivery — seven
days is the common choice — with the buyer able to open a dispute during that
window to stop the clock. That belongs in the reconciliation worker, which is not
built yet.

**Do not hide this at the defense.** Naming it, explaining the fix, and saying why
it was not built in the time available is a much better answer than being asked
"what if the buyer never confirms?" and discovering the answer live.

### I4. How would someone defraud this system?

| Attack | Does the current design stop it? |
|---|---|
| Seller takes payment and never ships | **Yes** — money is in escrow; the seller was never paid |
| Seller ships a worthless item | **Partly** — the buyer can refuse to confirm, but resolving who is right needs an admin and a dispute flow |
| Buyer receives the item and falsely claims it never arrived | **No** — this is the mirror risk, and the platform has no evidence either way without delivery tracking |
| Buyer never confirms, out of malice or apathy | **No** — see I3 |
| Buyer and seller collude to move money through the platform | **No** — no velocity limits or pattern detection |
| Same payment webhook replayed to credit twice | **Yes** — deterministic idempotency key; a duplicate produces no second transaction |
| Two concurrent withdrawal requests for the same balance | **Yes** — row locking; tested with a concurrent test that asserts only one succeeds |

The pattern in that table is worth stating out loud: **the system is solid where
the failure is mechanical and weak where the failure requires judgement.**
Concurrency, replay, and arithmetic are handled. Deciding who is telling the truth
is not, because that needs a dispute process and there is not one.

### I5. What is the commission, and does the business work?

Currently seeded at **5%**, stored in `settings.platform_fee_percent` and read at
release time, so it is configurable without a deploy. Fee arithmetic is in basis
points on integers and rounds down, which means rounding error favours the seller.

Whether 5% is *right* is untested — it is assumption **A5** in the problem
discovery register, and survey Q11 is the question that tests it. Listing on Chợ
Tốt and Facebook is free, so the commission has to be worth the protection it buys.

### I6. What is measured to know if this works?

Not signups. The metric that matters is whether trades **complete**:

| Metric | Why it is the right one | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Escrow release rate — escrows released ÷ escrows created | Directly measures whether the core promise resolves | `escrows` table |
| Median hours from delivery to buyer confirmation | The friction point in the whole model; also sizes the auto-release window | `orders`, `escrows` |
| Refund rate | The failure mode escrow exists to catch | `ledger_transactions` |
| Stuck escrows — holding for over 14 days | Directly measures the I3 hole | `escrows` |
| Repeat sellers | Whether the delayed-payment trade-off is acceptable in practice (tests A3) | `orders` |

All five are queries against the production database, not an analytics product
that has to be integrated.

### I7. Why build a double-entry ledger instead of a balance column?

Because a balance column is a number two requests can write at the same time, and
when it is wrong there is no way to find out how it got wrong. A ledger makes the
balance *derived*: it is the sum of the entries, the entries are append-only, and
every transaction is balanced by construction.

Concretely, it is what makes these possible: the same PayOS webhook arriving three
times produces one credit; a release that fails half-way leaves no partial money;
and if a total ever looks wrong, the entries say exactly which transaction caused
it.

This is the strongest engineering decision in the project and the one most worth
explaining unprompted.

### I8. What is deliberately not being built?

Ratings and reputation, delivery tracking integration, a dispute-resolution
workflow, seller identity verification, and promoted listings. Each is defensible
to omit in a capstone timeframe — but note that **the dispute workflow is the one
whose absence weakens the product story**, since the pitch is about what happens
when a trade goes wrong.
